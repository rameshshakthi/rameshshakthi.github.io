Chapter 04

Marketing

Marketing is not just advertising — it's identifying customer needs, then creating, promoting, selling, delivering, and continuing to service the product. This chapter covers the agency relationship, the three types of agent authority, the major marketing systems, producer compensation, and the rules that keep selling insurance honest.

41 — More Than Advertising

What is insurance marketing?

Marketing helps an insurer determine which products meet customer needs, then creates, promotes, sells, and delivers them — and it continues after the sale through customer service.

Identify customer needs
↓
Create products
↓
Promote → Sell → Deliver
↓
Continue serving the customer
42 — Distribution Matters Too

Why marketing matters

Mari's insurer could have a product that's properly priced, well designed, useful, and financially sound — but if nobody knows it exists, that means few sales. A good insurance product still needs an effective distribution and marketing system.

43 — Terminology

Who is a producer?

The chapter uses agent, broker, producer, solicitor, and sales representative. The book generally uses producer and agent interchangeably for someone who sells insurance for one or more insurers — but there are important legal distinctions between them, covered below.

44 — The Core Relationship

The agency relationship

An agency relationship exists when one party, the agent, represents or acts on behalf of another party, the principal.

InsurerPrincipal
→
Agentrepresents insurer
→
Customer
Example

Jai works as an agent for XYZ Insurer. XYZ is the principal; Jai is the agent, performing authorized activities on XYZ's behalf.

45 — Formalizing It

The agency contract

The relationship is normally formalized through a written agency contract, which can specify:

  • Scope of authority and products the agent may sell
  • Binding authority
  • Compensation and handling of funds
  • Termination provisions
46 — L-O-R-A-R

Five duties to the principal

DutyMeaningExample
LoyaltyAct for the principal's benefit within the legal/contractual frameworkRamesh should not help a customer hide facts from XYZ to get a policy issued
ObedienceFollow the principal's lawful instructionsSabari cannot bind homeowners coverage beyond his authorized limit
Reasonable careAct as a reasonably prudent person wouldAn agent shouldn't carelessly give obviously incorrect coverage advice — risk of E&O exposure
AccountingProperly account for the principal's money and propertyAn agent can't treat a customer's ₹50,000 premium as personal spending money
Relaying informationKeep the principal informed of relevant factsJai's agent learns an insured building is now vacant and must relay that to the insurer
Memory — L-O-R-A-R

Loyalty, Obedience, Reasonable care, Accounting, Relaying information. Because the agent represents the insurer, information the agent knows can be legally attributed to the insurer in appropriate circumstances.

47 — It Goes Both Ways

Principal's duties to agent

  1. 1Pay compensation — commission, salary, or other agreed pay
  2. 2Indemnify the agent, in appropriate circumstances, if the agent suffers loss or liability without fault while acting within authorized duties
48 — Professional Risk

E&O — errors and omissions

Example

Mari asks Sabari, an agent, "do I have coverage for this business exposure?" Sabari incorrectly says yes. A loss later occurs and the policy doesn't cover it — Mari suffers financial harm and may bring an E&O claim against Sabari.

Memory

E&O = a professional mistake that can cause customer harm.

50 — Spelled Out & Bridging the Gap

Express authority, binding authority & binders

Express authority is specifically granted by the agency contract — e.g. "Jai may bind homeowners coverage up to ₹50 lakh."

Binding authority is the power to make coverage effective on behalf of the insurer. When Ramesh calls his agent needing immediate coverage, the agent's binding authority lets him properly bind it right away.

Application / agreement
↓
Binder — temporary coverage
↓
Formal policy issued

A binder can be written or oral, and bridges the gap until formal policy documentation is issued — as when Mari's agent says "coverage is bound" the moment she buys a new car.

51 — Reasonably Necessary

Implied authority

Authority reasonably necessary to accomplish the agent's authorized purpose — even if not specifically stated.

Example

XYZ expressly authorizes Ramesh to sell policies but never explicitly says he may collect premiums. Since collecting premiums is reasonably necessary to complete the authorized sale, that can be implied authority.

Memory

Express = specifically stated. Implied = reasonably necessary.

52 — What It Looks Like to Others

Apparent authority

Exists when the principal's actions make it reasonable for a third party to believe the agent has authority.

Example

XYZ gives Jai branded forms and binding authority for routine policies. XYZ later terminates Jai but doesn't retrieve the materials or notify customers. Mari sees Jai using XYZ materials and reasonably believes he still has authority — if she relies on that appearance, XYZ may be bound under apparent authority principles.

Memory

Apparent = authority as it appears to the third party.

53 — All Three Together

Three authorities, one table

TypeEasy meaningExample
ExpressSpecifically grantedContract says the agent may bind coverage
ImpliedNecessary to perform the jobCollecting premiums to complete a sale

Super-memory

EXPRESS = contract says it. IMPLIED = job requires it. APPARENT = customer thinks it.

55 — Choice Among Insurers

Independent agency system

An independent agency generally represents several unrelated insurers. It's its own business, and its producers are generally employed by the agency rather than by the insurers.

Insurer A
Insurer B
Insurer C

↓ all reachable through the independent agency ↓

Customer

Key feature

The agency typically owns its expiration list.

56 — Who's Up for Renewal

The expiration list

Contains current policyholders and their policy expiration dates — telling the agency whose policy is coming up for renewal, and when.

Insurer A becomes uncompetitive
↓
Agent checks Insurer B
↓
Offers alternative, subject to the customer's approval

Ownership of the expiration list is a major distinguishing feature of the independent agency system.

57 — A Common Confusion

Independent agent vs. broker

Independent agentBroker
Generally represents insurersRepresents the insurance buyer / customer

Ramesh wants business insurance; a broker shops among insurers to find suitable coverage and value for him. Brokers are not legal representatives of insurers and generally don't have the same binding authority as agents.

58 — The Wholesaler Layer

Managing general agency (MGA)

An MGA is an independent business that appoints and supervises independent agents for insurers using the independent agency system — functioning somewhat like a wholesaler.

Insurer
→
MGA
→
Agents
→
Customers

Its exact authority depends on its contracts with insurers, and MGAs typically receive a managerial commission — often called an override — based on premium or profitability.

59 — One Insurer Only

Exclusive agency system

An exclusive agent sells insurance for one insurer or a group of related insurers, and can't normally shop freely among unrelated insurers. The agent is generally self-employed rather than an employee, though arrangements vary.

60 — Head to Head

Independent vs. exclusive

FeatureIndependentExclusive
Insurers representedUsually several unrelated insurersOne insurer / group of related insurers
FlexibilityHigherLower

Memory

Independent = independent choice among insurers. Exclusive = exclusive relationship with one insurer/group.

61 — Insurer as Employer

Direct writing system

Sales representatives are employees of the insurer — unlike the independent or exclusive agency setup, where the producer runs their own business. Direct-writing representatives may be called agents, but they're employees, and the insurer generally owns the expiration list.

62 — Beyond the Agent

Alternative distribution channels

Direct response

No agent is involved — sales happen through mail, phone, or internet. Sabari clicks an online ad → gets a quote → applies → pays → receives a policy, with no traditional agent necessary.

Internet

The web touches multiple points — online quotes, billing, policy issuance, customer service — and web-based distributors can act as portals/aggregators delivering leads to insurers.

Call centers

Handle sales, general inquiries, claims-related processing, billing questions, and endorsements — like Ramesh calling in to change his address.

Group marketing

Sells to members of a common organization — affinity, mass, or worksite marketing. When Mari's employer offers group insurance, premiums may be deducted through payroll.

Financial institutions

Banks and other institutions distribute insurance too, often through an insurance representative or a strategic arrangement with an insurer.

63 — Blending Channels

Mixed marketing systems

Many insurers don't rely on just one channel — combining independent agents, internet, call centers, and direct response into a mixed marketing system is increasingly common.

Example

Sabari can buy through an agent, get a quote online, call the insurer, or renew digitally — the same insurer supports all of these channels.

Commissions can apply to both new policies and renewals, subject to the applicable arrangement.

66 — Keeping Producers on Track

Producer supervision

Insurers can't just appoint producers and forget them — marketing representatives, agency managers, district managers, and field/regional offices supervise and support them to maintain relationships and motivate profitable volume.

67 — Selling the Right Business

Producer motivation

Insurers want producers selling the right products + profitable business — through encouragement, bonuses, commissions, sales contests, and recognition. Motivation is part of marketing management, not "sell anything at any price."

68 — Listening to the Field

Product management & development

Marketing gathers information from producers, customers, competitors, and claims experience, then works with other departments on coverage, pricing, services, and modifications.

Customer need
↓
Producer feedback → Marketing
↓
Product development → new/modified coverage

When Sivaprakasam notices customers asking about a new technology-related exposure, he can pass that demand up through marketing — people in sales are often the first to spot changing customer needs and competitive developments.

69 — Selling Legally

Producer regulation & licensing

State insurance departments regulate both insurers and producers — mainly through licensing and unfair trade practice laws. Requirements vary by state, but generally include examination, qualifications, pre-licensing education, insurer appointment, and continuing education.

Education → licensing exam → license
↓
Insurer appointment → sell insurance
↓
Continuing education → renewal
Important distinction

Passing the licensing exam isn't necessarily enough — a producer may also need an insurer appointment before selling for that insurer, depending on the state's requirements.

70 — Keeping Sales Honest

Unfair trade practices

PracticeExample
Misrepresentation / false advertisingSabari tells a customer "this policy covers every possible business loss" when it clearly has exclusions
Tie-in salesA dealership says "you can only get the car loan if you buy insurance from my insurer" — insurance improperly required as a condition of another transaction
RebatingRamesh offers to secretly return part of his commission to a customer to induce a sale, where prohibited by law
Other deceptive practicesFalse statements about a competitor, false information on an application, or omitting a fact that would get an application rejected, to earn a commission
Memory

Never sell the policy that exists only in your imagination. Insurance transactions rely on honesty and utmost good faith — state rules on rebating differ, so always follow the applicable legal context.

Chapters 3 + 4 — Connected

Marketing brings the business in; financials tell you if it worked

Marketing → customers → policies → premiums
↓
Underwriting → claims
↓
Financial results → profitability + solvency
Example

Ramesh's insurer runs an aggressive campaign and sells 100,000 new policies. Fantastic? Not necessarily — if the risks were poorly selected or mispriced: more policies → more exposure → more claims → higher loss ratio → higher combined ratio → potential financial stress. Marketing and financial performance are directly connected.

Four Case Studies

Real-world scenarios worth remembering

Ramesh's Insurance Company — reading the ratios together

Written premiums ₹11 crore, earned premiums ₹10 crore, incurred losses + LAE ₹7.5 crore, underwriting expenses ₹3.3 crore, net investment income ₹1 crore, surplus ₹5 crore.

StepCalculationResult
Loss ratio₹7.5cr ÷ ₹10cr75%
Expense ratio₹3.3cr ÷ ₹11cr30%
Combined ratio75% + 30%105% — underwriting is losing money
Investment income ratio₹1cr ÷ ₹10cr10%
Overall operating ratio105% − 10%95% — favorable overall
Capacity ratio₹11cr ÷ ₹5cr2.2 : 1 — monitor, don't judge alone

Conclusion: underwriting is weak, investment income is helpful, the overall operation is positive — exactly the integrated thinking Chapter 3 wants you to build.

Mari's Marketing Journey — a policy's life through distribution

Mari contacts an independent agent, who compares Insurer A, B, and C, gathers information, and submits the application. The insurer evaluates the business (that's underwriting — Chapter 5), the policy is issued, and the agency services it. At renewal, the agency reviews her needs again — marketing continuing well beyond the initial sale.

Sabari and Apparent Authority

XYZ terminates Sabari's agency but never retrieves his branded materials. Sabari accepts Mari's application on XYZ documents and tells her coverage is bound; Mari reasonably believes he still has authority. If a loss occurs, apparent authority principles may bind XYZ depending on the facts — the principal can create legal consequences through what it makes appear authorized, not just what it explicitly grants.

Jai and E&O

Ramesh asks agent Jai what coverage his software business needs. Jai carelessly says "your basic policy covers all professional errors." Ramesh later faces a professional liability claim the policy doesn't cover — incorrect advice → customer reliance → coverage gap → financial harm → potential E&O claim. A producer's professional responsibility matters.

16 Exam Traps

The most important Chapter 3 & 4 traps

Chapter 3
Trap 1

✗ Written premium = earned premium.

✓ Written is recorded when the policy is written; earned occurs as coverage is provided.

Trap 2

✗ Paid losses = incurred losses.

✓ Incurred = paid + change in reserves.

Trap 3

✗ Loss ratio uses written premiums.

✓ Loss ratio uses earned premiums.

Trap 4

✗ Expense ratio uses earned premiums.

✓ Expense ratio uses written premiums.

Trap 5

✗ Combined ratio includes investment income.

✓ Investment income is considered separately.

Trap 6

✗ A combined ratio below 100% means overall profit.

✓ It generally indicates only an underwriting gain — overall performance also depends on investment results.

Trap 7

✗ Assets minus all liabilities = surplus.

✓ It's specifically admitted assets minus liabilities.

Trap 8

✗ The balance sheet shows annual profitability.

✓ Balance sheet = position at a point in time; income statement = performance over a period.

Chapter 4
Trap 9

✗ An agent represents the customer.

✓ Agent → insurer. Broker → customer.

Trap 10

✗ An independent agent represents one insurer.

✓ Independent agents generally represent several unrelated insurers.

Trap 11

✗ An exclusive agent is necessarily an insurer employee.

✓ Exclusive agents are generally self-employed, though arrangements vary.

Trap 12

✗ A direct writing rep owns the expiration list.

✓ The insurer generally owns it.

Trap 13

✗ Express and apparent authority are the same.

✓ Express = actually granted. Apparent = appears granted to a third party.

Trap 14

✗ Implied authority is written in the contract.

✓ It arises from what's reasonably necessary to accomplish the authorized purpose.

Trap 15

✗ Marketing ends after the policy sale.

✓ Marketing includes continuing customer relationships and post-sale service.

Trap 16

✗ An MGA is just another name for an insurance company.

✓ An MGA is an intermediary that appoints/supervises independent agents, subject to its contracts with insurers.

One Building, Five Floors

The big picture: Chapters 1 through 5

1

What is insurance? Risk management + Transfer + Business + Contract.

2

Who provides it, and who regulates it? Insurers + Government + Regulation.

3

Is the insurer financially healthy? Profitability + Solvency + Ratios.

4

How does it reach customers? Marketing + Agents + Brokers + Distribution.

5

How does it decide which risks to accept? Underwriting — next chapter.

Once this chain is clear, AINS-21 stops feeling like hundreds of disconnected definitions and starts behaving like one insurance company doing one giant job — marketing brings the business in, underwriting decides what to accept, claims pays when something goes wrong, and finance keeps score.
