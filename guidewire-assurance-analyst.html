Section 01

What Is Guidewire?

Guidewire is a software platform used by Property & Casualty (P&C) insurers to manage insurance operations.

Its InsuranceSuite includes major applications such as:

  • PolicyCenter: policy administration
  • BillingCenter: billing and payments
  • ClaimCenter: claims management
  • PricingCenter: pricing/rating capabilities
  • UnderwritingCenter: underwriting workflows and risk assessment

Guidewire describes InsuranceSuite as a connected core for the insurance lifecycle.

The simplest mental model

Think:

Customer
   |
   v
PolicyCenter -----> BillingCenter
   |
   v
ClaimCenter

PolicyCenter manages the policy lifecycle.

BillingCenter manages the money side.

ClaimCenter manages what happens after a loss.

Example

A customer buys auto insurance:

  1. Agent creates an account.
  2. Agent starts a submission.
  3. PolicyCenter collects risk information.
  4. Rating calculates premium.
  5. Underwriting rules evaluate the risk.
  6. Customer accepts the quote.
  7. Policy is bound.
  8. Policy is issued.
  9. BillingCenter handles invoices and payments.
  10. Customer has an accident.
  11. ClaimCenter manages the claim.

This entire chain is the foundation of your Guidewire understanding.

Section 02

P&C Insurance Foundation

Property

Property insurance protects against losses involving property.

Examples:

  • Home
  • Building
  • Equipment
  • Commercial property
  • Personal belongings

Casualty

Casualty insurance generally covers liability and other non-property exposures.

Examples:

  • Auto liability
  • General liability
  • Workers' compensation
  • Professional liability

Line of Business (LOB)

A Line of Business is a category of insurance.

Examples:

  • Personal Auto
  • Homeowners
  • Commercial Property
  • Commercial Auto
  • General Liability
  • Workers' Compensation

Why LOB matters in Guidewire

Different LOBs have different:

  • Products
  • Coverages
  • Rating factors
  • Underwriting rules
  • Forms
  • Workflows
  • Data requirements
  • Integrations

For example, a Personal Auto product needs vehicle and driver data. A Homeowners product needs property-related information.

Section 03

Insurance Policy Basics

A policy is the insurance contract between the insurer and insured.

A policy commonly contains:

  • Named insured
  • Effective dates
  • Coverages
  • Limits
  • Deductibles
  • Premium
  • Exclusions
  • Conditions
  • Forms/endorsements

Premium

The premium is the amount charged for the insurance.

Example:

Annual premium = ₹30,000

Coverage

Coverage describes what is protected.

Example:

Collision coverage = Yes
Comprehensive coverage = Yes
Liability coverage = Yes

Limit

The limit is the maximum amount payable under a coverage, subject to policy terms.

Example:

Coverage limit = ₹5,00,000

Deductible

The deductible is the amount the insured pays before the insurer pays the covered portion, subject to policy terms.

Example:

Covered loss = ₹1,00,000
Deductible = ₹20,000
Potential insurer payment = ₹80,000

Section 06

What Is a Job in PolicyCenter?

A job is a policy transaction being processed.

This is one of the most important terms for project discussions.

Common job types include:

  • Submission
  • Renewal
  • Policy Change
  • Cancellation
  • Reinstatement
Example

Existing policy:

Honda
₹25,000 premium

Customer changes vehicle:

Honda -> Toyota

This is generally handled through a:

Policy Change job

If a developer says:

"The defect occurs only on Renewal jobs."

They mean the problem happens during the renewal transaction, not necessarily during new submissions or policy changes.

Section 07

Submission

A submission represents a request/application for insurance.

Example:

A customer wants a new auto policy.

The agent enters:

  • Driver information
  • Vehicle information
  • Location
  • Coverage selections
  • Prior insurance
  • Loss history

This creates the information needed to evaluate and quote the risk.

Section 08

Quote

A quote represents the price and terms offered for the risk.

Example:

Vehicle: Honda City
Driver age: 35
Coverage: Comprehensive
Premium: ₹28,000

Quote is not the same thing as bind.

Section 09

Rating vs Underwriting vs Bind vs Issue

This distinction is critical.

Rating

Question:

How much should the policy cost?

Example:

Driver age
+
Vehicle
+
Location
+
Coverage
+
Other rating factors
       |
       v
Premium = ₹32,000

Underwriting

Question:

Should the insurer accept this risk, and under what terms?

Example:

Driver age < 21
       |
       v
Underwriting referral

Underwriting can include risk selection, review, referral, acceptance, rejection, or other actions depending on the insurer's rules.

Bind

Bind is the action that makes the quoted insurance contract legally binding.

Bound

Bound is the resulting state/status.

A useful mental model:

Submission
   |
Quote
   |
Underwriting / requirements
   |
Bind
   |
Bound
   |
Issue

The exact workflow and timing of underwriting checks can vary by implementation.

Important

Do not memorize:

Bind = Underwriting

Instead:

Underwriting evaluates the risk. Bind commits the coverage. Bound is the resulting status.

Section 10

Issue

Issuing is the process of finalizing the policy after binding according to the insurer's configured workflow.

It can involve:

  • Final policy record
  • Policy documents
  • Forms
  • Endorsements
  • Downstream integrations
  • Billing information

Some Guidewire workflows can bind and issue together, while others can separate them.

Section 11

Policy Change / Endorsement

A policy change modifies an existing policy during its term.

Examples:

  • Add vehicle
  • Remove vehicle
  • Change address
  • Change driver
  • Add coverage
  • Remove coverage
  • Change limits
  • Change deductible

Example:

Existing vehicle value = ₹10L
New vehicle value = ₹15L

Policy Change effective = 01-Jul-2026

The system may need to:

  1. Update vehicle data.
  2. Recalculate rating.
  3. Re-evaluate underwriting.
  4. Update premium.
  5. Update billing.
  6. Generate forms/documents.
  7. Send integrations.

This is why a small requirement can affect many components.

Section 12

Effective Date vs Transaction Date

This is a frequent source of defects.

Transaction date

When the transaction is processed.

Effective date

When the change actually takes effect.

Example:

Today: 15-Jun-2026
Customer requests vehicle change effective: 01-Jul-2026

The transaction happens on June 15.

The policy change becomes effective July 1.

Do not automatically treat these dates as the same.

Testing example

If a renewal is processed on:

15-Dec-2026

but the current policy ends:

31-Dec-2026

the next policy period may begin:

01-Jan-2027

If the system incorrectly starts the renewal on December 15, that is a date/transaction defect worth investigating.

Section 13

Business Rules

Business rules define behavior based on business conditions.

Example:

IF driver age < 21
THEN create underwriting referral

Another:

IF comprehensive coverage is selected
AND vehicle age exceeds configured threshold
THEN require underwriting review

A business rule is not simply a field validation.

Section 14

Validation vs Business Rule

Validation

Checks whether data is valid/acceptable.

Examples:

  • Required field cannot be blank.
  • Vehicle value must be greater than zero.
  • Date cannot be earlier than policy start date.

Business rule

Determines what business action should occur.

Example:

IF driver age < 21
THEN refer to underwriter

Memory trick

Validation = Is the data acceptable?

Business rule = What should the business do?

Section 15

Rating

Rating is the calculation of premium.

A simplified example:

Vehicle value = ₹15L
Driver age = 35
Location = Chennai
Coverage = Comprehensive
Claims history = Clean

              |
              v
            Rating
              |
              v
Premium = ₹32,000

Real rating can be much more complex.

As an Assurance Analyst, you should ask:

  • Which factors affect the premium?
  • What are the expected values?
  • What happens at boundary values?
  • Does a policy change trigger re-rating?
  • Does the effective date affect rating?
  • Does the API return the same premium?
  • Does BillingCenter receive the same premium?
Section 16

Underwriting Referral

A referral occurs when the system determines that human review is required.

Example:

Driver age = 19
        |
        v
Underwriting rule
        |
        v
Referral Required
        |
        v
Underwriter reviews

A good test is not merely:

"Did the referral message appear?"

You also test:

"Can the agent bind before the referral is resolved?"

If binding is prohibited by the requirement and the system allows it, that is a functional/business-rule defect.

ClaimCenter

A simplified flow:

Claim Created
 |

 |

 |

 |
Evaluation
 |

FNOL

First Notice of Loss.

Example:

Customer reports an accident.

Reserve

An estimate of the amount the insurer may ultimately need to pay for the claim.

Section 24

UI vs Business Logic vs Data

Suppose the UI shows:

Premium = ₹0

But the API response says:

Premium = ₹30,000

Possible problem:

  • UI mapping
  • UI transformation
  • Display logic
  • Incorrect field binding

Now suppose the API response itself says:

Premium = ₹0

while PolicyCenter's underlying expected calculation is ₹30,000.

Then investigate deeper:

  • Rating
  • Input data
  • Product configuration
  • Business rules
  • Backend processing

The key is:

Trace the value through the system.

Section 28

Forms in Guidewire

This is particularly relevant because you heard your teammate discussing a Variable Form.

First distinction: UI form vs insurance form

A UI form is a screen/data-entry structure.

An insurance policy form is part of the policy's contractual/document package.

Guidewire PolicyCenter documentation states that the physical representation of an insurance policy is a collection of policy forms. Forms define aspects such as coverages, exposures, exclusions, and regulatory requirements.

Important:

PolicyCenter stores information identifying forms through form patterns. The actual physical forms are generally maintained in an external documentation/form management system.

Section 29

Form Pattern

A form pattern is the configuration/identification information PolicyCenter uses to determine a particular form.

A form pattern can include information such as:

  • Code
  • Form number
  • Edition
  • Name
  • Product applicability
  • Job types
  • Inference time
  • Jurisdiction availability
  • Replacement information
  • Endorsement numbering behavior

Guidewire documentation states that forms can be automatically applied at a configured stage such as quote or bind.

Section 30

Why Forms Matter

Suppose an insurer has:

Personal Auto Product

A policy might require different forms based on:

  • State/jurisdiction
  • Coverage
  • Product
  • Transaction type
  • Effective date
  • Endorsements
  • Other policy characteristics

Therefore, the system needs to determine:

Which forms apply to this policy?

Section 31

Variable Form Concept

The term Variable Form can be project-specific, so do not assume every Guidewire project uses the exact same implementation.

Conceptually, think of a variable/dynamic form as a document template whose values depend on policy data.

Example:

Template:

Named Insured: {CustomerName}
Policy Number: {PolicyNumber}
Vehicle: {Vehicle}
Premium: {Premium}

For one customer:

Named Insured: Ramesh
Policy Number: PA10001
Vehicle: Honda City
Premium: ₹25,000

For another:

Named Insured: Kumar
Policy Number: PA10002
Vehicle: Toyota
Premium: ₹32,000

The template can be similar while the populated values change.

Section 33

Form Edition

A form may have different editions.

Example:

Form ABC
Edition 2025
Edition 2026

A new edition may be required because:

  • Regulatory wording changed
  • State requirement changed
  • Product wording changed
  • Insurer updated language

Testing must consider effective dates and availability.

A common defect pattern is:

Correct form number, wrong edition.

Section 34

Form Inference

Form inference is the mechanism by which Guidewire determines whether a form should be applied.

Examples of generic inference concepts in current PolicyCenter documentation include forms that are:

  • Always added
  • Added based on coverage/clauses
  • Added based on additional insured/interests
  • Added or removed based on policy changes
  • Selected based on coverable types

You do not need to memorize the technical class names.

Understand:

Inference = deciding whether/how a form applies to the policy.

Section 35

Forms Testing Checklist

When testing forms, ask:

Selection

  • Is the correct form selected?
  • Is it applicable to the product?
  • Is it applicable to the jurisdiction?
  • Is it applicable to the transaction?

Timing

  • Is it applied at quote?
  • Bind?
  • Issue?
  • Policy change?

Version

  • Correct form number?
  • Correct edition?
  • Correct effective dates?

Content

  • Correct customer name?
  • Correct policy number?
  • Correct vehicle?
  • Correct coverage?
  • Correct limits?
  • Correct premium?
  • Correct effective date?

Transaction

If a policy change occurs:

  • Is a new form generated?
  • Is an endorsement form generated?
  • Is an old form removed/replaced if required?
Section 36

Documents vs Forms

These are not necessarily the same thing.

Guidewire documentation distinguishes them.

Policy form

Represents contractual parts of the insurance policy.

Document

An electronic file associated with an account, job, policy, claim, etc.

Examples of PolicyCenter documents can include:

  • Uploaded photographs
  • Inspection reports
  • Correspondence

A document may exist alongside the policy forms.

Section 37

Testing Types

Functional testing

Does the feature work according to the requirement?

Example:

Adding a vehicle should update the policy.

Integration testing

Do systems exchange the correct information?

Example:

PolicyCenter sends ₹32,000 to BillingCenter.

UI testing

Does the UI behave/display correctly?

Regression testing

Did a new change break something that already worked?

Negative testing

Does the system correctly handle invalid input?

Example:

Vehicle value = ₹0

If requirement says value must be greater than zero, the system should reject/prevent the transaction.

Boundary testing

Test values around a business threshold.

Example:

Under 21 -> Referral
21 -> ?
22 -> Normal

Always verify the exact requirement for the boundary.

Section 38

Expected vs Actual

Every serious test should distinguish:

Expected

What should happen.

Actual

What happened.

Example:

Expected:
Vehicle change triggers rating.

Actual:
Vehicle changed but premium remained unchanged.

Result:

FAIL

This is the foundation of defect reporting.

Section 40

A Practical Defect Example

Requirement:

When vehicle value changes from ₹10L to ₹15L, premium must be recalculated.

Observed:

UI vehicle = ₹15L          ✓
PolicyCenter vehicle = ₹15L ✓
Rating = ₹32K              ✓
API response = ₹32K        ✓
BillingCenter = ₹25K       ✗

Likely investigation area:

PolicyCenter → BillingCenter integration.

Do not waste the first hour debugging the rating calculation when rating is already correct.

Section 41

Severity vs Root Cause

These are different.

Root cause

Where/why the defect occurred.

Severity

How seriously it affects the business/system.

Example:

A premium mismatch in BillingCenter may be high impact because it can affect billing accuracy.

But severity should be determined according to the project's defect-management process.

As an analyst, do not automatically assign severity based only on technical difficulty.

Section 43

What You Should Put in Acceptance Criteria

Bad requirement:

"Add vehicle capability."

Better:

Given an active auto policy
When the agent starts a Policy Change
And adds a new vehicle
Then the vehicle should be saved
And applicable rating should be recalculated
And applicable underwriting rules should execute
And the premium should reflect the effective date
And applicable forms should be generated
And BillingCenter should receive the updated premium

This is much easier to test.

Section 45

  • Screenshots

Section 47

What You Need to Know About Gosu

You do NOT need to become a Gosu developer immediately.

You should recognize that Gosu is a programming language used in Guidewire development/configuration.

At your level, learn to recognize:

  • Variables
  • Conditions
  • Loops
  • Functions/methods
  • Classes
  • Entities
  • Queries
  • Rules

Example concept:

if driver.Age < 21 {
    createReferral()
}

You don't need to memorize syntax.

You need to understand what the code is doing.

Section 48

Section 56

The Most Important Analyst Skill

Do not memorize Guidewire as a dictionary.

Learn to trace behavior.

Whenever someone describes a requirement or defect, ask:

WHAT
 ↓
WHERE
 ↓
WHEN
 ↓
WHY
 ↓
EXPECTED
 ↓
ACTUAL
 ↓
IMPACT
 ↓
ROOT CAUSE

Example:

"Premium is wrong."

Do not immediately ask:

"Which code is broken?"

Ask:

What premium was expected?
What premium was calculated?
What inputs were used?
Which transaction?
What effective date?
Did rating run?
What did the API return?
What did BillingCenter receive?
What did the UI display?

That is how you move from "Guidewire learner" to "project-ready analyst."
