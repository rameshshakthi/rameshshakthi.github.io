Chapter 06

Claims

01 — The Job Behind the Payout

What is a claim?

A claim is a demand for payment or performance under an insurance policy after a loss. Sabari's job as a claim representative isn't simply "pay the customer" — every claim has to be properly analyzed first.

  1. 1Verify coverage
  2. 2Determine cause of loss
  3. 3Determine amount of loss
  4. 4Settle or deny the claim

05 — A Classic Exam Trap

Peril vs. hazard

TermMeaningExample
PerilThe cause of lossFire, theft, collision, flood
HazardSomething that increases the frequency or severity of lossPoor wiring is a hazard; the fire it eventually causes is the peril
Trap

Peril and hazard are not the same thing. Peril = cause. Hazard = condition that makes the cause more likely or more severe.

06 — Two Ways a Policy Can Be Built

Named perils vs. special form

Named perils policies list the specific covered causes of loss. If the cause isn't on the list, there's generally no coverage.

PerilCovered?
Fire✓
Theft✓
Wind✓
Flood✗

Special form policies generally cover direct loss unless an excluded peril applies.

Direct loss
↓
Initially potentially covered
↓
Insurer checks exclusions
↓
Excluded peril? YES → deny · NO → continue coverage analysis
Burden-of-proof memory

Named perils — the insured generally must show the cause was a covered peril. Special form — the insurer generally must establish an applicable exclusion to deny coverage on that basis.

07 — The Insured's Share

Deductible

A deductible is the portion of a covered loss the insured absorbs before the insurer pays the rest.

Loss ₹1,00,000 − Deductible ₹10,000 = ₹90,000 insurer payment

If the loss is below the deductible, the insurer generally pays nothing.

Why deductibles exist

  1. 1Reduce small claims
  2. 2Encourage care
  3. 3Reduce claim-handling costs
  4. 4Help keep premiums reasonable
08 — What Is the Loss Worth?

Valuation

MethodMeaning
Actual Cash Value (ACV)Replacement Cost − Depreciation. Example: a new machine costing ₹10 lakh, with ₹3 lakh depreciation, has an ACV of ₹7 lakh.
Replacement CostCost to replace with new property of like kind and quality, subject to policy conditions
Agreed ValueValue agreed between insurer and insured when the policy is written
Replacement Cost ₹10,00,000 − Depreciation ₹3,00,000 = ACV ₹7,00,000
09 — Two Degrees of Damage

Partial loss vs. total loss

A partial loss occurs when only part of the property is damaged. A total loss occurs when property is completely destroyed, or is treated as a total loss under the applicable policy rules.

Example

Mari owns a ₹50 lakh building. A fire causes ₹8 lakh of damage — that's a partial loss. Valuation and policy limits still matter either way, whether the loss is partial or total.

↓
Business income loss
Example

A fire damages Mari's supermarket. She loses the building, inventory, and equipment — but she also loses income during the shutdown and spends money trying to keep operating elsewhere. Property coverage addresses the physical damage; business income coverage can address the covered income loss and extra expenses, per the policy.

12 — Two Ways an Insurer Recovers

Subrogation vs. salvage

TermMeaningExample
SubrogationThe insurer seeks recovery from a responsible third party after paying a covered lossAnother driver damages Mari's car through negligence. Her insurer pays her, then pursues the responsible driver.
SalvageThe damaged property itself still has remaining valueA severely damaged vehicle can still be sold for parts
Memory

Subrogation = recover from the person responsible. Salvage = recover value from the damaged property.

4 Exam Traps

The most important Chapter 6 traps

Trap 1

✗ Peril = hazard.

✓ Peril = cause of loss. Hazard = something that increases the frequency or severity of loss.

Trap 2

✗ ACV = replacement cost.

✓ ACV = Replacement Cost − Depreciation.

Trap 3

✗ Subrogation = salvage.

✓ Subrogation → recovery from the responsible third party. Salvage → value from the damaged property.

Trap 4

✗ A public adjuster represents the insurer.

✓ A public adjuster represents the insured.
